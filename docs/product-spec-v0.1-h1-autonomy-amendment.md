# AI4Math Chronicle — Product Specification v0.1 H1 Autonomy Amendment

**Status:** Ratified by active trusted maintainer
**Approved by:** `@Charlie-Wang-03`
**Approved on:** 2026-11-19
**Amends:** [Frozen Product Specification v0.1](./product-spec-v0.1.md) §§31–33, solely as to autonomous historical-significance decisions and the editorial publication boundary

## Product decision

AI4Math Chronicle remains **timeline-first, evidence-backed** and historically selective. Significance tiers H1/H2/H3, canonical YAML Event records, bilingual content, S1–S5/E0–E4 source and evidence semantics, verification/formal assurance, inclusion boundary, static architecture, and GitHub reviewability do **not** change.

AI maintainers may now autonomously assign and materially change **H1** (as already permitted for H2/H3), subject to the rigorous significance rationale, sourcing, attribution, uncertainty and PR/CI discipline of the [Governance H1 Autonomy Amendment](./gpt-project-governance-h1-autonomy-amendment.md).

This **supersedes** the original §32 ban on autonomous final H1 decisions and makes a **narrow exception** to the §33 `autonomous publishing` non-goal: qualified H1/H2/H3 Event changes may be merged after protected PR/CI without individual human editorial sign-off. Unexamined scanner output, direct mass ingestion and unvalidated publication remain prohibited.

## Weekly corpus maintenance

A weekly ChatGPT task is approved to examine a rolling seven-day interval, plus material evidence updates to existing leads; deduplicate and assess candidates, record qualified H1/H2/H3 Events, preserve unresolved source leads, and report outcomes. The detailed method is [Weekly Chronicle Loop](./weekly-chronicle-loop.md). There is no weekly publication quota. This is **not** a new news product, newsletter, backend, scheduled GitHub crawler, or project phase.

## Unchanged human decisions

`independently_verified`, `disputed`, `corrected` as current status, `retracted`, and material mathematical-correctness, priority or attribution adjudication remain human-gated, as do unrelated product, governance, phase and destructive-action changes.
